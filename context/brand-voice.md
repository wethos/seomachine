# Wise Step Recruiting Brand Voice & Messaging

This document defines the Wise Step Recruiting brand voice, tone, and messaging framework. Reference this when writing all content to ensure consistency.

*Generated for Wise Step Recruiting — August 3, 2026*

## Brand Voice Pillars

### 1. Sharp & Anti-Hype
- **What it means**: Declarative and confident, but grounded in specifics rather than adjectives. Every claim is backed by a concrete number, a named fact, or a source — never an unsupported adjective.
- **How it sounds**: Plain but authoritative. Short, punchy lines carry the argument; longer sentences do the explaining. If a sentence is exciting but empty, it's wrong.
- **Example**: "Romania used to be a cost play. In 2026, it's a speed play."
- **Avoid**: Hype adjectives ("insane," "game-changing," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge"). Generic filler ("best-in-class," "world-class solutions," "passionate about people"). Any claim without a number or named fact behind it.

### 2. Engineer-Credible
- **What it means**: Authority earned by reminding the reader that Wise Step's founders are engineers themselves. This is technical recruitment done by people who understand the discipline — not generic HR.
- **How it sounds**: Written in "we" voice. Technical terms (stack names, role titles) used freely where relevant, but no jargon for its own sake.
- **Example**: "This is not generic HR. It is technical recruitment done by people who understand the discipline."
- **Avoid**: Sounding like a generalist staffing agency. Jargon used to impress rather than clarify.

### 3. Honest About Uncomfortable Truths
- **What it means**: Willing to name uncomfortable truths (tax changes, standard counter-offers) instead of selling a clean story. "Honest, no surprises, no radio silence."
- **How it sounds**: Direct address ("if you're hiring…"). Opens by overturning a lazy assumption, then backs the correction with hard numbers.
- **Example**: "AI handles the scale. Our team handles the judgement. Both matter. Neither replaces the other."
- **Avoid**: Selling a clean story that hides the friction. Salesy or hyped framing.

### 4. Playbook-Shaped & Actionable
- **What it means**: The reader leaves with something they can use — a benchmark, a timeline, a screening question, a decision.
- **How it sounds**: Content structured as tables, timelines, and numbered lists the reader can act on. Serves both SEO and AEO.
- **Example**: "Eastern Europe used to be the 'cheaper option.' In 2026, it's the option that can actually fill your roles."
- **Avoid**: Vague feel-good language that could belong to any agency. Content with no takeaway.

## Tone Guidelines

### General Tone: Sharp, honest, data-backed, credible, anti-hype
Declarative and confident, but grounded in specifics rather than adjectives. You sound like former software engineers who now do technical recruitment — you know the discipline, you name the uncomfortable truths, and you back every claim with a number or a named fact. Applies consistently across content types (blog and outreach) — no separate voice per channel.

### Tone Variations by Content Type

**Blog posts**: Playbook-shaped, first-hand, sharp
- Follows structural cues — tables, timelines, numbered lists
- Default to a reversal/hook opener when no other structure fits ("used to be X — now it's Y")
- Published in both English and Romanian

**Outreach / Email**: Same baseline standards, no exceptions
- Every factual claim sourced or backed by a concrete number
- No hype, no "family" tone

**LinkedIn**: Hook-first, mixed structure
- Hook-first opener, then bullets and prose mixed as needed
- No emoji-driven LinkedIn-guru energy

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Vary length**: Mix short, punchy lines that carry the argument with longer sentences that do the explaining.
- **Active voice preferred**: "Our team handles the judgement" not "the judgement is handled by our team"
- **Clarity first**: No jargon for its own sake; technical terms only where they earn their place.

### Paragraph Structure
- **One idea per paragraph**: Keep the point clear and singular.
- **Playbook-shaped**: Break arguments into tables, timelines, and numbered lists wherever possible.
- **White space**: Favor scannable structure the reader can act on.

### Word Choice
- **Backed, not adjective-driven**: "40% of counter-offers" not "amazing retention"
- **Plain but authoritative**: Write like an engineer explaining to a peer.
- **Direct address**: "if you're hiring…"
- **Cut the fluff**: If a sentence is exciting but empty, it's wrong.

### Terminology

**Say This** → **Not That**
- "speed play" → "cost play" (the market moved — reflect it)
- "curated, not bulk" → "high volume of candidates" (quality over quantity)
- "AI handles the scale; we handle the judgement" → "AI-powered hiring" (name the division of labor)
- "former software engineers" → "recruitment experts" (earn authority with the specific fact)
- "no surprises, no radio silence" → "great communication" (concrete over vague)

## Content Formatting

### Headlines
- **Reversal hook**: "used to be X — now it's Y" is the strong default when no other structure fits.
- **Specific over vague**: Name the market, the year, the number.
- **No hype words**: Never "insane," "game-changing," "revolutionary."

### Subheadings
- **Actionable**: Signal what the reader will be able to do or decide.
- **Playbook cues**: Frame sections as benchmarks, timelines, checklists.

### Lists
- **Act-on-able**: Every list should give the reader something to use.
- **Tables/timelines/numbered lists**: Preferred structure for both SEO and AEO.

### Calls-to-Action
- **Honest framing**: No hype, no false urgency.
- **Concrete next step**: Tie the CTA to a benchmark, decision, or timeline.

## Voice Examples

"Eastern Europe used to be the 'cheaper option.' In 2026, it's the option that can actually fill your roles. We're former software engineers, so when we screen a candidate for your stack, we're judging the same things you would in a technical interview. AI handles the scale — sourcing, matching, the first pass. Our team handles the judgement. Both matter. Neither replaces the other. And we'll tell you the uncomfortable parts too: the standard counter-offer, the tax change that shifts the math. Honest, no surprises, no radio silence."

**Why this works**:
- Opens by overturning a lazy assumption, then backs the correction
- "We" voice earns authority by naming the engineering background
- Names uncomfortable truths instead of selling a clean story
- Every claim is concrete, not an unsupported adjective
- Reader leaves knowing exactly how the process divides between AI and people

### Not Wise Step Voice ❌

"We're a passionate family of recruiters on an incredible journey to revolutionise hiring with our insane, game-changing talent solutions! 🚀 We're best-in-class, world-class, and we're passionate about people."

**Why this fails**:
- Corporate "we're a family" culture-speak
- Hype adjectives with nothing behind them ("insane," "game-changing," "revolutionise")
- Generic filler that could belong to any agency
- Emoji-driven LinkedIn-guru energy
- Zero sourced claims, zero actionable takeaway
