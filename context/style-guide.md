# Wise Step Recruiting Style Guide

This guide defines writing conventions, formatting standards, and editorial guidelines for all Wise Step Recruiting content.

Pairs with `brand-voice.md`. Where voice defines *what we sound like*, this defines *how we format it*.

---

## Grammar & Mechanics

### Capitalization

**Headlines & Subheadings**:
- **Decision**: Sentence case (only capitalize first word + proper nouns). Keeps the sharp, anti-hype tone; Title Case reads as corporate marketing.

**Product / Brand Names**:
- Wise Step: always capitalized, two words
- Wise Step Recruiting: full name on first reference, "Wise Step" after

**Industry Terms**:
- recruitment / recruiting: lowercase unless starting a sentence
- developer / engineer: lowercase
- role titles when generic (backend engineer, DevOps lead): lowercase
- named stacks / tools (React, Kubernetes, PostgreSQL): match official casing
- AI: always caps

### Numbers

**When to Spell Out**:
- Spell out: one through nine
- Use numerals: 10 and above
- Exceptions — always numerals:
  - Percentages (5%, 50%)
  - Money (€5, €500 — see currency below)
  - Time-to-hire, salaries, headcount, any benchmark stat
  - Lists / stats (scannability)

**Large Numbers**:
- 1,000+ uses comma
- Spell out million, billion (1.2 million)
- Salaries: €5,000 or €5K both acceptable; prefer €5,000 in prose, €5K in tight table cells

**Currency**:
- Default EUR (€) for market/salary figures; specify RON or USD explicitly when the source data is in those.
- Symbol before the number: €4,500

### Punctuation

**Oxford Comma**:
- **Decision**: Yes (A, B, and C). Removes ambiguity in claim-dense sentences.

**Em Dashes**:
- Style: — (em dash, no spaces)
- Usage: parenthetical statements or emphasis — a signature move in our voice
- Example: "Romania — for years the cost play — is now the speed play."

**Quotation Marks**:
- **Decision**: Curly quotes ("…" and '…') in published prose. Straight quotes only in code.

**Ellipses**:
- Three dots, no surrounding spaces, used sparingly (mainly omitted text in quotes)

### Abbreviations & Acronyms

**First Use**:
- Spell out on first use, acronym in parentheses
- Exceptions for universally known: SEO, AEO, AI, URL, CTA, HR, EU

**Common Recruitment Acronyms**:
- ATS: Applicant Tracking System
- IC: Individual Contributor
- TTH: Time to Hire
- OTE: On-Target Earnings
- EOR: Employer of Record
- (Add others as needed)

**Latin Abbreviations**:
- **Decision**: Generally avoid e.g. / i.e. / etc. in favor of plain English ("for example," "that is," "and so on").

## Word Choice & Usage

### Preferred Terms

**Say This** → **Not That**:
- speed play → cost play (the market moved — reflect it)
- curated / shortlisted → bulk candidates
- technical recruitment → generic HR / staffing
- candidate → resource / headcount (when talking about people)
- former software engineers → recruitment experts (earn authority with the fact)
- judgement → gut feel
- honest / no radio silence → great communication

### Words to Avoid
- Hype adjectives: "insane," "game-changing," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "exciting"
- Corporate fluff: "best-in-class," "world-class," "passionate about people," "we're a family"
- Empty intensifiers: "very," "really," "actually," "simply"
- Passive constructions where active is clearer
- "click here" / "read more" (use descriptive links)
- Any claim with no number, named fact, or source behind it

### Inclusive Language
- Gender-neutral throughout ("they" not "he/she")
- "candidate," "engineer," "developer" instead of gendered terms
- Avoid idioms that don't translate — content ships in English and Romanian
- Be mindful of accessibility in descriptions

## Formatting Standards

### Text Formatting

**Bold**:
- Use for key benchmarks, takeaways, the load-bearing claim in a paragraph
- Don't overuse — if everything is bold, nothing stands out
- Example: "**Time to hire in Romania dropped to 18 days** in Q2 2026."

**Italics**:
- Emphasis (sparingly), titles of works, foreign phrases
- Example: "The Romanian term *dezvoltator* just means developer."

**Underline**:
- Avoid — reserve for links

**ALL CAPS**:
- Avoid except standard acronyms (SEO, AI, URL)

### Lists
Playbook-shaped content is core to our voice — lists, tables, and timelines are preferred structure, not decoration.

**Bulleted Lists**:
- Non-sequential items, benchmarks, criteria
- Capitalize first word
- Period only if the item is a complete sentence; no period on fragments
- Parallel structure (all sentences or all fragments)

**Numbered Lists**:
- Sequential steps, rankings, ordered priorities
- Format: `1.` `2.` `3.`
- Same capitalization / punctuation rules as bullets

**Tables**:
- Preferred for comparisons, benchmarks, salary bands, market shifts
- Header row in sentence case
- Keep cells terse — this is where numerals and €5K short forms belong

**Nested Lists**:
- Max 2 levels deep

### Links

**Anchor Text**:
- Descriptive — tell the reader where they're going
- Keyword-rich when natural, 2–5 words
- ✅ "our 2026 Romania salary benchmarks"
- ❌ "click here"

**Link Formatting**:
- External links open in a new tab; internal links same tab

### Code & Technical Elements

**Inline Code**:
- Backticks for tool names in technical context, file names, snippets
- Example: "Screen for real `React` + `TypeScript` depth, not buzzword bingo."

**Code Blocks**:
- Multi-line code / config only; include language identifier

### Callout Boxes / Asides

**When to Use**:
- Uncomfortable truths worth flagging (tax changes, standard counter-offers)
- Benchmarks or a sharp one-line takeaway

**Format**:
- Blockquote (`>`) for the callout
- Lead with a bold label when useful: "**Reality check:** the standard counter-offer lands within 48 hours."

## Content Structure

### Article Introduction
**Standard Structure**:
1. **Hook** (1–2 sentences): default to the reversal opener — "used to be X — now it's Y" — when no stronger structure fits
2. **Correction / stakes** (2–3 sentences): overturn the lazy assumption, back it with a number
3. **Promise** (2–3 sentences): the benchmark, timeline, or decision the reader leaves with
4. **Credibility** (optional): remind them we're engineers who've done the work

**Keyword Placement**:
- Primary keyword in the first 100 words, naturally integrated

### Section Length
- Ideal: 250–350 words per main section
- Break anything past ~500 words into subsections
- Every section should hand the reader something actionable

### Conclusion
**Standard Structure**:
1. **Recap**: key takeaways (bullets or short paragraph)
2. **Action**: the concrete next step / decision
3. **CTA**: honest framing, no false urgency
4. **Forward-looking** (optional): one sharp line on where the market is heading

## SEO & AEO-Specific Style

Content serves both SEO and AEO (answer engines / AI overviews). Structure for direct-answer extraction: clear questions as headings, tight declarative answers underneath.

### Meta Titles
- 50–60 characters including spaces
- Include primary keyword
- Append "| Wise Step" if space allows
- No ending punctuation

### Meta Descriptions
- 150–160 characters including spaces
- Include primary keyword and a concrete hook (a number, not an adjective)
- End with a complete thought

### URL Slugs
- Lowercase, hyphen-separated
- Include primary keyword, 3–5 words
- Format: `/blog/primary-keyword-phrase`
- Romanian variants: mirror the slug in Romanian on the RO version

### Alt Text
- Describe what the image shows, keyword naturally if relevant
- 125 characters or less
- No "image of" / "picture of"

## Bilingual Publishing (EN / RO)

- Primary content ships in both English and Romanian.
- Translate meaning, not words — the sharp, anti-hype voice must survive the translation.
- Keep named stacks, role titles, and tool names in their original form in both languages.
- Localize benchmarks and currency context (RON vs EUR) where the audience differs.
- Match slug, meta, and heading structure across both language versions.

## Dates & Time

**Date Format**:
- **Decision**: Month DD, YYYY (August 4, 2026) in English; DD month YYYY in Romanian.

**Time**:
- **Decision**: 24-hour format (15:00). Common across EU audience.

**Time Zones**:
- Specify when relevant, EET/EEST for local market ("15:00 EET")

## Statistics & Data

Core rule from the voice: every factual claim is sourced or backed by a concrete number. This section is non-negotiable.

### Citing Sources
- Always cite statistics with a source
- Format: "According to [Source], [statistic]."
- Link to the original source when possible
- Include the year: "In 2026, time to hire for backend roles…"
- Never publish a statistic or claim we can't stand behind

### Presenting Numbers
- Round large numbers for readability: "1.2 million" not "1,234,567"
- Use the % symbol (15%), not "percent"
- Use the € symbol for money (€500), not "euros"
- Include commas: 10,000 not 10000

## Images & Media

### Image Captions
- **Decision**: Optional — only when they add context.
- Sentence case, period if a complete sentence, placed below the image

### Screenshots
- Crop to the relevant portion; add arrows/highlights to draw attention
- Alt text describes what's shown

### Charts & Graphs
- Use when they clarify a benchmark or trend
- Simple, clean design; provide context in surrounding text
- Alt text describes the key finding, not just "chart"

## Brand-Specific Guidelines

### Wise Step References
- "Wise Step" (not "the Wise Step platform")
- "We" voice throughout — we're former software engineers, and the content should keep earning that authority

### Competitor References
- **Decision**: Name competitors directly only when factual and relevant; otherwise use generic terms ("generalist staffing agencies").
- Be fair and factual, no disparaging language
- Differentiate on substance (engineer-led screening, speed, honesty), not criticism

## Accessibility

### Screen Reader Friendly
- Descriptive link text (not "click here")
- Alt text on all images
- Proper heading hierarchy (H1→H2→H3, no skipping)

### Plain Language
- Plain but authoritative — write like an engineer explaining to a peer
- Define non-obvious technical terms on first use
- Short sentences and paragraphs; break complex ideas into digestible chunks

## Voice & Tone Reminders

See `brand-voice.md` for the full framework.

### Core Voice Characteristics
1. Sharp and anti-hype
2. Data-backed — every claim sourced
3. Engineer-credible ("we're former software engineers")
4. Honest about uncomfortable truths
5. Playbook-shaped and actionable

### Tone Variations
- **How-to / playbook content**: instructive, benchmark-driven, actionable
- **Strategic content**: authoritative, first-hand, decisive
- **Market / trend content**: sharp, analytical, reversal-opener friendly
- **Outreach**: same baseline standards — no hype, no exceptions

## Editing Checklist

Before publishing any content:

**Grammar & Mechanics**:
- [ ] Spelling and grammar checked
- [ ] Punctuation correct (Oxford comma, em dashes, curly quotes)
- [ ] Numbers and currency formatted consistently
- [ ] Dates formatted correctly (per language)

**Style**:
- [ ] Sentence-case headings
- [ ] Preferred terminology used, banned words absent
- [ ] Consistent formatting (bold, italics, lists, tables)
- [ ] Links are descriptive

**Structure**:
- [ ] Strong hook (reversal opener when nothing stronger fits)
- [ ] Playbook-shaped where possible (tables, timelines, numbered lists)
- [ ] Proper heading hierarchy
- [ ] Conclusion with concrete next step + honest CTA

**SEO / AEO**:
- [ ] Keyword integrated naturally, in first 100 words
- [ ] Meta title/description optimized
- [ ] Structured for direct-answer extraction
- [ ] Internal + external links included, images have alt text
- [ ] Slug optimized; RO version mirrored

**Quality (deal-breakers)**:
- [ ] Every statistic sourced and one we can stand behind
- [ ] No hype adjectives, corporate fluff, or "family" tone
- [ ] Could only have been written by Wise Step — not a generic agency
- [ ] Reader leaves with a benchmark, timeline, screening question, or decision
- [ ] No broken links; ready for publication
